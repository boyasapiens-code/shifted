Replacing a good team member costs you weeks of hiring, training, and lost consistency — far more than the raise that would have kept them. Here's what actually drives them out, and what to do about it.

## Unpredictable schedules

People have lives. Posting the roster two days out, or changing it last-minute, is the number-one quiet reason good staff start looking. Publish further ahead and protect it.

## Feeling invisible

Most people don't leave over money first — they leave because no one noticed the work. A specific "that save during the rush was excellent" costs nothing and lands harder than a generic thank-you.

## No path

Your strongest people want to know what's next — shift lead, trainer, a new skill. If there's no visible step up, they'll find one somewhere else.

## Money you won't talk about

Vague pay and unexplained deductions breed resentment fast. Put the service-charge split in writing, show it on the payslip, and pay on time without being chased.

## What good looks like

Stable schedules, real recognition, a path, and honest money. None of it is expensive. All of it is the difference between a team that stays and a permanent hiring problem.

## The quick version
Thailand's minimum daily wage in 2026 runs from 337 to 400 baht, depending on the province.
Bangkok is at 400 baht. Importantly for our industry: hotels (mid-size and up) and
entertainment venues pay the 400-baht rate nationwide, wherever they are. Service charge is
separate — it's not legally required and it's not part of minimum wage.

## What the law says
- Minimum wage is set **per day, by province**, by the Wage Committee. For 2026 the range is roughly 337–400 THB/day.
- The top 400 THB rate applies to Bangkok, Phuket, Chonburi, Rayong, Chachoengsao, and Koh Samui — **and to type-2/3/4 hotels and entertainment venues across the whole country** (this widened on 1 July 2025).
- Minimum wage is a **floor**. No employee may be paid below the rate for their province or sector, whether paid daily or monthly.
- Service charge (the typical 10%) is **not** set by law. A venue may add it only if it's clearly displayed to customers. How it's split is up to the venue.

## Both sides
### For employees
- Check your province's rate — and note the hotel/entertainment rate is 400 THB even outside the big provinces.
- Your base pay can't drop below minimum wage. Service charge is on top, not instead.
- Ask how service charge is calculated and split. You're entitled to a clear answer.

### For employers
- Audit your rates now if you run a hotel or entertainment venue outside Bangkok — you may owe 400 THB.
- Decide a service-charge policy and write it down. Display it to customers; explain the split to staff.
- Underpaying minimum wage is one of the fastest routes to a labour complaint and back-pay order.

## Myths & gray areas
- **"Service charge counts toward minimum wage."** No. Minimum wage is base pay; service charge is extra.
- **"Tips and service charge are the same thing."** No. A tip is voluntary and goes to staff. Service charge is a venue-added fee the venue controls.
- **"Monthly-salary staff aren't covered."** They are — divide salary by working days to confirm it meets the daily floor.

## What good looks like
The venues that keep staff longest are transparent about money. Put the service-charge
split in writing, show it on payslips, and pay at or above the floor without being chased.
It costs nothing and removes the single most common source of staff resentment.
